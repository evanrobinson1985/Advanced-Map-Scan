Source: USGS 3DEP bare-earth Digital Terrain Model (ground-classified LiDAR returns, forest canopy stripped) — not a canopy-top surface model. 1-meter resolution is the absolute minimum for detecting human-scale anomalies like middens or small cave vestibules.
Ready to load data.
Downloads everything in the map view, in tiles that are fetched in parallel and stitched into one seamless DTM. Auto uses full 1 m LiDAR whenever the whole view fits the memory budget, and otherwise the finest resolution that fits. The terrain is drawn with smooth bicubic interpolation, so zooming in past the data's resolution stays smooth instead of turning blocky. Finer pixels than the source LiDAR are interpolated and don't add real detail.

Method: iMound (Freeland et al. 2016). The terrain is detrended and flipped upside down, and the "pits" a fill has to raise are the mounds, measured from their own base contour, so only closed highs count, not hillside bulges or ridge noses. Each candidate is then re-tested with random 3DEP-grade LiDAR error added (stochastic depression analysis) and must persist in most runs. Finally it must correlate with an ideal conical dome or flat-topped platform (template matching, Davis et al. 2018).

Caves: Priority-Flood depression fill (closed-basin/doline detection). Mounds: Local Relief Model via morphological opening (regional-trend removal, radius set by Trend Radius above), scanned at three scales and merged since one radius alone can't see both small and large mounds well. Shelters: flags flat ground at the base of a real nearby cliff - not the overhang itself, which no bare-earth elevation model can see, the same reason a horizontal cave mouth can't be seen either. Waterways are derived straight from the DEM (flow accumulation, same technique as ArcGIS/QGIS hydrology tools) and excluded unless a candidate is an obviously deep pit or large opening despite the overlap - real sites still get a modest boost for sitting near (not on) a waterway, since they cluster near water. All results are algorithmic candidates — verify in the field. Click the map any time to inspect elevation, depth/relief, and to confirm a feature it did or didn't find.

5. Training Data

Confirmed: 0 cave/sinkhole, 0 mound example(s)
Confirming a cave/sinkhole or mound — the checkbox on a marker's popup, or "This is a Cave/Sinkhole" / "This is a Mound" from click-to-inspect — only ever widens thresholds to include it and raises similar future candidates' Match Score. It never narrows the search or excludes anything unlike what's been confirmed so far. Settlement confirmation works differently, on purpose: a camp or village site can't be verified by looking at the map the way a hole in the ground can, so "Confirm Known Site" asks what actual evidence you have (artifacts, a documented record, midden staining, oral history) rather than offering a one-click checkbox. Confirmed sites boost future scans two ways: areas near a confirmed site score higher, and areas whose water/elevation/slope profile resembles a confirmed site score higher too, even far away. Each mode is learned separately. Training data lives in this browser only (localStorage) — export it to back it up or move it to another device.
